The problem

A new team touches four places by hand, and they drift apart.

Each step lives in a different tool, owned by a different person. Six months later nobody can say which subnet, node pool, route and rule belong together — or to whom.

1 · A CIDR request

A ticket for free address space, a spreadsheet to check it against, a subnet per zone created by hand.

2 · Subnet tags

The tags Karpenter and the load balancers select on, typed again on every subnet, and wrong on one of them.

3 · NodeClass and NodePool

A Karpenter EC2NodeClass and NodePool per team, copied from the last team's, with its taint and its subnet selector.

4 · A VPN route and a rule

A route on the subnet router, a rule in the tailnet policy file, edited in the admin console by whoever is around.

Access wider than needed

The quick fix is a rule for the whole VPC. Every team can reach every other team's nodes, and nobody notices.

Whose route is this?

A route nobody can map to a team stays forever, because removing it might break someone. Leaving teams leave holes.

What one object does

Five steps, in order, each a condition you can read.

Every step waits for the one before it and reports a condition with a reason and a message. Ready is True when all five are.

01 · SUBNETS

A subnet per zone

SubnetsReady

A SubnetClaim to the Subnet Operator: one free /22 (or the size asked) in each zone, tagged hs/team.

02 · ISOLATION

A team security group

IsolationReady

Ingress only from the team's own CIDRs, the cluster and the VPN. A taint on the nodes and a NetworkPolicy for the pods.

03 · NODES

Karpenter nodes

NodesReady

An EC2NodeClass and NodePool on those subnets and that group — or, in TagOnly, just the tags for a team's own Karpenter.

04 · ROUTE

A VPN route

RouteReady

Checked on your existing subnet routers (external, the default), or advertised by a Connector of its own (connector).

05 · ACCESS

Access for the group

AccessGranted

A grant from the team's group to exactly its CIDRs: proposed for a person to paste (default), or written by the operator.

Access is granted last and revoked first. A team never gets a rule for a route that does not work yet; on deletion the grant goes before the route, the nodes, the security group and finally the subnets.
Each failure stops the line where it happens. A CIDR outside every approved route leaves RouteReady=False NotCovered, with the CIDR and the route in the message, and no access at all.

Route modes

Reuse the subnet routers you have, or give a team its own.

Either way the route only makes the CIDRs reachable over the VPN. Who may use it is decided by the access grant, which is per team.

DEFAULTroute.mode: external

External: check, change nothing

An existing EC2 subnet router — ideally two, in different zones — advertises one broad route, such as the whole VPC 10.20.0.0/16, once.

  • The operator changes nothing on the routers.
  • It checks via the Tailscale API that a device with the configured tag (tag:subnet-router) advertises an approved route that covers every team CIDR.
  • A CIDR outside it, say in a secondary block 10.31.0.0/16, is RouteReady=False NotCovered.
  • Team separation comes from the access grant, not from the route.
TeamNetworkClassspec.vpn.route
mode: external
external:
  deviceTag: tag:subnet-router
  routerSecurityGroup: sg-0e7f8091a2b3c4d5e
route.mode: connector

Connector: one per team

An in-cluster Tailscale operator Connector per team advertises exactly the team's CIDRs, no more.

  • For CIDRs no shared router covers, or when a team's route must be its own.
  • Replicas and the device tag per team; the routes it advertises are the team's subnets.
  • Removed with the team, after its access.
TeamNetworkspec.vpn.route
mode: connector
connector:
  replicas: 2
  tag: "tag:hs-team-data"
Access approval

Propose by default. Write only when you say so.

The grant is always the same shape: the team's group, the team's CIDRs, nothing else.

DEFAULTapproval: propose

Propose: a person pastes it

The operator never writes the tailnet policy file. It shows the exact grant to paste between // hs:team-network begin and // hs:team-network end, in the object's status and in the dashboard, and reports AccessProposed.

  • Fits a policy file kept in Git and reviewed like code.
approval: auto

Auto: only its own marked section

The operator writes the grants between the markers through the Tailscale API, and nothing outside them.

  • Validates the new policy with Tailscale first.
  • Writes with If-Match on the ETag it read, so a concurrent edit is never overwritten.
  • No markers in the file: AccessGranted=False MarkersMissing, and it writes nothing.

dstThe TeamNetwork's own CIDRs, never the VPC, never another team's.

Safety and isolation

A team gets its own network, and nothing more.

Three layers of isolation

A security group that admits only the team's CIDRs, the cluster and the VPN; a taint so only the team's pods land on its nodes; a NetworkPolicy for the pods.

The grant names only its own CIDRs

The dst of a grant is always the TeamNetwork's own subnets, computed from its status, never typed by hand and never wider.

Only the marked section

In auto mode it writes between // hs:team-network begin and end and nowhere else; in propose mode it does not write the file at all.

Never 100.64.0.0/10

Subnets are never handed out from Tailscale's own address range, so a team CIDR can never collide with a VPN device address.

Only the platform writes TeamNetworks

RBAC gives the kind to the platform team. A team cannot widen its own access by editing its object.

Groups from an allow-list

The VPN group must be in the class's allowedGroups; any other group is refused before anything is created.

The object

A few lines per team. The rest is the class.

Both kinds are cluster-scoped. A TeamNetworkClass holds the platform's settings per environment, like an IngressClass; a TeamNetwork per team refers to it. API sketch: teams.hypersurgery.dev/v1alpha1.

teamnetwork-payments.yamlone per team
apiVersion: teams.hypersurgery.dev/v1alpha1
kind: TeamNetwork
metadata:
  name: payments
spec:
  classRef: prod
  team: payments  # hs/team tag, label, taint
  subnets:
    prefixLength: 22
    zones: [eu-central-1a, eu-central-1b]
  nodes:
    limits: {cpu: "64"}
  vpn:
    group: "group:payments"
    approval: propose       # propose (default) | auto
    route:
      mode: external       # external (default) | connector
teamnetworkclass-prod.yamlone per environment
apiVersion: teams.hypersurgery.dev/v1alpha1
kind: TeamNetworkClass
metadata:
  name: prod
spec:
  subnets:
    scopeRef: organization
    account: "222222222222"
    region: eu-central-1
    networkID: vpc-0a1b2c3d4e5f60718
    claimNamespace: team-networks
  isolation:
    clusterSecurityGroup: sg-0c1d2e3f405162738
  nodes:
    mode: Managed          # or TagOnly
  vpn:
    provider: tailscale
    tailscale:
      tailnet: example.com
      allowedGroups: ["group:payments", "group:search"]
    route:
      mode: external
      external: {deviceTag: "tag:subnet-router"}
How it compares

Good tools cover parts of this job.

OptionWhat it doesWhat's missing for this job
Crossplane · kro + ACK · Kratix composition The platform defines a "team onboarding" template and teams create instances. AWS promotes kro + ACK for EKS team onboarding. A toolkit, not the product: you write the non-overlapping CIDR allocation, the route coverage checks and the safe tailnet policy edits yourself.
Capsule multi-tenancy Tenant = team: namespaces, quotas, policies. Inside the cluster only. No AWS subnets, nodes or VPN.
Tailscale Kubernetes operator A Connector turns the cluster into a subnet router. Doesn't manage per-team access rules; those stay manual or in Git.
Terraform AWS + Tailscale providers Subnet, Karpenter and Tailscale rule in one module. Runs once and doesn't reconcile. Team removal and drift are caught by hand.
Twingate · Teleport · StrongDM resource access Group-based access to individual resources. A different product that replaces the VPN. They don't do subnets or nodes.

Team Network Operator is designed to own the whole chain in one object and remove it cleanly in reverse; to get non-overlapping CIDRs from the Subnet Operator; to propose VPN rules instead of writing them, unless you choose auto; to check that the EC2 router's route really covers the team's subnets; and to keep an audit of who opened access to what.

You can assemble this with a composition tool. You would then write the allocation, the route checks and the policy-file edits yourself.
